# Foresight-Phys benchmark — critical analysis

Date: 2026-05-15
Reviewer: Claude (Opus 4.7)
Run set analysed: `docs/{gpt-5.4, gpt-5.4-mini, gpt-5.4-nano, gpt-5.5}-…/benchmark_results.json` and `.cache/analysis/{predictions,scored,per_field}.parquet` (output of `foresight-phys-analyze`).

> Update 2026-05-15 (post-fix v2). `analyze.py` now (i) paper-macros the cross-model summary (one paper = one vote, experiments macro-averaged within each paper), (ii) drops infinite log-accuracy rows from the per-model mean, and (iii) reports a multi-threshold accuracy continuum (factor-2 / factor-3 / factor-10) instead of the single 0.5-score (factor 3.16) cutoff. The substring-based leakage detector was removed wholesale — it was producing more noise than signal (see commit history; the previous §2.4 below is preserved as a methodological footnote). The plot pipeline is now split into a paper-macro view (`aggregate_paper_macro`, `per_paper`, `paper_dominance`, `numeric_thresholds`) and a per-field-micro view (`aggregate_per_field`, `difficulty_distribution`, `numeric_log_ratio_cdf`, `numeric_scatter`, `model_agreement`). All numbers below come from the regenerated `docs/analysis/summary.json` and `.cache/analysis/{scored,per_field}.parquet`.

> TL;DR. The pipeline measures **something** real — ranking lines up with model capability, models clearly beat trivial baselines on numeric prediction (paper-macro `frac_within_factor_2`: gpt-5.5 0.69 vs constant baseline 0.31; `frac_within_factor_3`: 0.80 vs 0.44), and inter-model agreement (median |log10 ratio| 0.16) is much tighter than disagreement with truth (0.25–0.42), suggesting a shared physics prior rather than memorised answers. The headline numbers in the per-run report were already paper-balanced; the cross-model `summary.json` is now paper-balanced too. What remains are real concerns about the test mix: one paper supplies 35 % of all scored fields and is heavily redundant in the difficulty histogram; the loosest threshold (factor ≈ 3.16, the implicit cut for "correct") is generous enough that a "predict ≈ 1" baseline already lands within a decade of 59 % of GTs; and many bool / categorical questions are essentially textbook outcomes restated. On the ~28 % of items where intuition has to do real work, models routinely miss by a full decade.

## 1. What the setup actually measures

Per `src/foresight_phys/benchmark.py` and `src/foresight_phys/system_prompt.txt`, the pipeline:

1. Loads each `JSONs/filtered/<arXiv>.json` (a list of "experiment" objects).
2. Replaces every `experiment_results.*.result` with `"TO_PREDICT"`.
3. Sends one Responses-API request *per experiment* with the "elite experimental physicist" system prompt, asking the model to fill the masked fields.
4. Scores per-field:
   - **numeric** (`float`/`integer`): `score = 1 − min(|log10(pred / gt)|, 1)`; "correct" iff `score ≥ 0.5`, i.e. **within a factor of 10^0.5 ≈ 3.16**.
   - **bool / categorical**: exact match after normalisation.
   - **formula**: whitespace-equivalent match, else judged by `gpt-5.4-nano`.
5. Aggregates across experiments → per-paper → per-run.

Field inventory (from `per_field.parquet`):

| type | n | notes |
|---|---|---|
| float | 296 | dominated by one paper (see §2.1) |
| bool | 19 | GT is `True` for 13 / 19 (majority baseline = 0.68) |
| categorical | 18 | k ∈ {2, 3, 4, 5}; mean 1/k baseline = 0.31 |
| integer | 7 | trivially handled by all models (6/7 trivial) |
| formula | 7 | judged by an LLM judge — same family as the test models |
| **total** | **347** across 20 papers, 70 (paper, experiment) groups |

Cross-model overlap is complete: every model produced a prediction for every field.

## 2. Setup-level concerns

### 2.1 One paper supplies 35 % of all fields
File `2602.15115` (CMS top–antitop spin-density tomography) contributes **120 / 347 = 35 %** of all scored fields. The next-largest paper has 24. The 120 fields are not 120 independent items — they are 5 quantum-information markers (`bell_correlation_marker_b`, `entanglement_marker_delta_e`, `magic_m2_tilde_bits`, `steering_marker_t`, `top_discord_bits`) crossed with 4 m(t̄t) bins × 3 |cos θ| bins = 60 bins per pass × 2 angle conventions.

The per-run `docs/<run>/benchmark_results.json::aggregate_*` numbers were already paper-balanced. The cross-model `docs/analysis/summary.json` previously was not; it now is, after `analyze.py` was switched to paper-macro of an experiment-macro. The headline `per_model` and `numeric_thresholds` blocks therefore now match the per-run aggregates:

| model | old per-field micro | new paper-macro (`summary.json`) | per-run `aggregate_prediction_quality` |
|---|---|---|---|
| gpt-5.4 | 0.683 | 0.715 | 0.715 |
| gpt-5.4-mini | 0.607 | 0.622 | 0.622 |
| gpt-5.4-nano | 0.630 | 0.625 | 0.625 |
| gpt-5.5 | 0.729 | 0.761 | 0.761 |

The new paper-macro spreads the four models slightly more (gpt-5.5 − gpt-5.4-mini: 0.122 → 0.139). Two parts of `summary.json` are still inherently per-field counts: `difficulty_distribution_overall` and `difficulty_by_type` are histograms over the field pool (one row per field, used to bucket "trivial / easy / hard / intractable"), so 35 % of those denominators still come from this one experiment. Per-difficulty model accuracy in §3.4 inherits the same per-field weighting and should be read with that in mind.

The plot pipeline now reports both views side-by-side: `docs/analysis/plots/aggregate_paper_macro.pdf` and `aggregate_per_field.pdf` show the same four bars under each weighting, and `paper_dominance.pdf` shows the with/without-`2602.15115` deltas under both weightings — making the size of the dominant-paper effect directly readable.

A single dominant paper also lets a single artefact (e.g. all four models converging on a wrong "prior" answer for asymptotic Bell markers) show up many times in the difficulty histogram.

### 2.2 The "correct" threshold is loose — now reported as a continuum
The single 0.5-score cutoff corresponds to factor 10^0.5 ≈ 3.16, which is generous: a "predict ≈ 1" baseline still satisfies it for 44 % of GTs (paper-macro `frac_within_factor_3 = 0.440`). `summary.json::numeric_thresholds` now reports three cutoffs side-by-side instead of collapsing on one:

| paper-macro fraction within … | factor 2 | factor 3 | factor 10 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gpt-5.4` | 0.674 | 0.819 | 0.924 |
| `gpt-5.4-mini` | 0.619 | 0.708 | 0.886 |
| `gpt-5.4-nano` | 0.622 | 0.749 | 0.890 |
| `gpt-5.5` | **0.691** | **0.805** | **0.938** |
| Constant baseline (geo-median ≈ 1.2) | 0.309 | 0.440 | 0.591 |

Read across rows, the model-vs-baseline gap holds at every cutoff but compresses sharply at factor-10 (gpt-5.5 lead over baseline: 0.38 / 0.37 / 0.35 dex). Read across columns, the implicit "correct" cutoff (factor ≈ 3) is the one where the four models look most similar — gpt-5.4-mini and gpt-5.4-nano are within 0.04 of each other at factor-3, but separate by 0.07 at factor-2 (gpt-5.4 leads gpt-5.5 by 1.3 pp on tight matches but trails by 1.4 pp at factor-3, suggesting different error tail shapes). For the public report the factor-2 column is the closest to what most experimental physicists would call "matching"; the factor-10 column is essentially "knew the order of magnitude". The pre-existing per-field-micro re-bucketing (factor 2 → 91/296 trivial, 65/296 intractable; factor 10 → 229/296 / 16/296) tells the same story under a different denominator and is reproduced as supporting evidence in the difficulty plot.

### 2.3 The numeric_log_accuracy_mean is no longer `Infinity` (was)
Previously, `per_model[*].numeric_log_accuracy_mean = Infinity` for every model — `_compute_log_accuracy` (in `build_dataset.py`) returns `inf` when a model predicts 0 for a non-zero GT, and `_per_model_summary` then averaged it straight in. `analyze.py` now strips `±inf` before paper-macroing, so the new values are well-defined: gpt-5.4 0.341, gpt-5.4-mini 0.400, gpt-5.4-nano 0.394, gpt-5.5 0.263 (one-decade bound, lower is better).

### 2.4 Leakage detection has been removed (methodological footnote)
The substring-based leakage flag (and the `leakage_impact` table it powered) has been **removed from the pipeline** as of this update. It was producing more noise than signal:

- `_leak` flagged a row if `str(gt)` appeared anywhere in the experiment description or result description. Among the 29 historically-flagged items, the GTs were short strings like `0.0`, `1.0`, `5.0`, `27`, `3` — incidental matches against numbers like "5.0 V gate voltage", "10 µJ pulse energy", "1600 nm laser".
- Concrete false positives included `concurrence_strong_dephasing_limit` (GT `0.0`, matched on any "0" in 200+ words of setup), `early_unshaped_alignment_feature_duration_ps` (GT `1.0`, matched on "10 % probe arm"), and `temporal_g30_zero_delay` (GT `0.5` — definitionally 0.5 for thermal light at order 3, not actual leakage).
- The paper-macro accuracy gap was small and inconsistent in sign across models (gpt-5.4-mini +5.8 pp; gpt-5.5 −12.9 pp), which is exactly what you would expect if the flag were tracking topic difficulty in the small `leak=True` subset (29 fields concentrated in 12 papers) rather than actual answer leakage.

To re-introduce leak-tracking properly we would either flag leakage at extraction time (the human curator marks "result value is verbatim in the description") or require a much stricter heuristic (numeric tokens not in a common-units stop-list *and* co-occurrence with the result key's words within a window). Until that exists, the pipeline simply doesn't try.

### 2.5 Bool / categorical fields are mostly textbook outcomes
Spot-checking all 19 bools: questions like "did coherent 1600 nm light show ATI rings?" (yes), "did coherent light show carpet-like interference?" (yes), "did the kicker store the beam?" (yes), "is g^(2)(0) = 1 for coherent state?" (yes — definitionally) are essentially recall checks against textbook physics. Model accuracy on bool is 0.79–0.95 against a 0.68 majority baseline; non-trivial signal but limited statistical power (n = 19) and biased toward "expected outcome".

Categorical fields are similarly modest: 18 fields, mean 1/k baseline = 0.31, models hit 75–94 % — but the within-paper k-distribution is uneven (paper `2601.11796` supplies most multi-class items with shared allowed-value sets across experiments).

### 2.6 Formula fields are scored by a same-family judge
7 formula items judged by `gpt-5.4-nano`. Two issues: (i) the judge is in the same model family as some of the candidates, which can introduce same-family bias; (ii) the formula bucket itself is too small (7) to support per-model conclusions — formula accuracy in `summary.json` is computed from a denominator that includes all formula counts, but the per-experiment averaging in `metrics.compute_file_metrics` first averages within experiments and then across, which makes the formula contribution to `prediction_quality` uneven across runs.

## 3. Do models have scientific intuition, or are they getting the easy ones?

### 3.1 They beat trivial baselines on numerics
Paper-macro numeric thresholds from the regenerated `summary.json` (one paper = one vote; experiments macro-averaged within each paper). The factor-3 column was added in this update; the factor-10 column was previously called "within decade":

| | median &#124;log10 (pred/gt)&#124; | within ×2 | within ×3 | within ×10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gpt-5.4` | 0.321 | 0.674 | 0.819 | 0.924 |
| `gpt-5.4-mini` | 0.365 | 0.619 | 0.708 | 0.886 |
| `gpt-5.4-nano` | 0.374 | 0.622 | 0.749 | 0.890 |
| `gpt-5.5` | **0.245** | **0.691** | **0.805** | **0.938** |
| Constant baseline (geo-median of all GTs ≈ 1.2) | — | 0.309 | 0.440 | 0.591 |

Two cross-checks against per-field micro statistics from the parquet (n = 289 fields where all four models predicted nonzero):

- "Always predict 1" baseline: median 0.680, within factor 2 = 0.25, within decade = 0.65.
- Per-paper geo-median baseline: median 0.343, within factor 2 = 0.47.

So the models are clearly above the constant-prediction baselines, and the gap is bigger under paper-macro than the old micro view (e.g. the constant baseline now lands within factor 2 only 31 % of the time once each paper gets equal weight, vs 21 % under the old micro that was held up by the dominant CMS paper's narrow value range). But the per-paper geo-median baseline already gets within factor 2 47 % of the time — a lot of "scientific intuition" credit is still "knowing the order of magnitude that this paper is operating in". The new factor-3 column makes the implicit "correct" cutoff visible alongside the tighter physics-relevant cutoff: gpt-5.5's lead over gpt-5.4 is **+1.7 pp** at factor-2 but only **−1.4 pp** at factor-3 (i.e. gpt-5.4 actually edges gpt-5.5 in that band) — a single-threshold report obscures this kind of structure.

### 3.2 Inter-model agreement is much tighter than agreement with truth
Pooled across model pairs:

| comparison | median &#124;log10 ratio&#124; |
|---|---|
| model vs **another model** | 0.16 |
| model vs **truth** | 0.25 |

Pairwise medians range 0.14–0.20. So the four models agree with each other more than with reality. This is consistent with a shared physics prior — they have learned to estimate orders of magnitude and conventions from training data — rather than a per-paper memorisation effect.

### 3.3 Trivial items live in a narrow magnitude band
For float fields:

- "trivial (all correct)" items: 95 % of GTs lie in [0.1, 100], 41 % in [0, 1], 32 % in [0.5, 1.5].
- "intractable (none correct)" items: only 47 % of GTs lie in [0.1, 100]; the std of log10|GT| is 3.55 (vs 0.91 for trivial).

Translation: "trivial" overlaps heavily with "the answer is on a scale a physicist's prior already covers" (visibilities, fidelities, branching ratios, dimensionless markers). "Intractable" is concentrated in answers that depend on device-specific scales (mobilities up to 4×10^6 cm²/V·s, loading rates of 10^11 s⁻¹, Bell-marker tails of 10⁻⁴) — exactly the cases where naive priors fail.

### 3.4 Where intuition would matter, models lose
Per-difficulty per-model accuracy (per-field micro — difficulty buckets are inherently per-field, so this view is unchanged by the paper-macro switch):

| difficulty | n | gpt-5.4 | gpt-5.4-mini | gpt-5.4-nano | gpt-5.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| trivial (all correct) | 177 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| easy (1 wrong) | 72 | 0.92 | 0.54 | 0.68 | 0.86 |
| discriminative | 35 | 0.63 | 0.31 | 0.37 | 0.69 |
| hard (1 right) | 25 | 0.16 | 0.12 | 0.12 | **0.60** |
| intractable (none correct) | 38 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |

`gpt-5.5` cleanly separates from the rest only on the "hard" tier (0.60 vs 0.12–0.16). On `intractable` the median model error is 1.06 in |log10|, i.e. **a factor of ~11 off** — these are the fields where actual scientific reasoning would distinguish a strong physicist from a fluent guesser, and *no* model in the current set crosses the threshold.

Sample intractable failure modes (representative, not cherry-picked):

- **`xi_ab_t_8p5nm_nm = 110`** (in-plane GL coherence length of a thin superconducting film): all four models predict 13–28 nm, the bulk-like value. None grasp the geometric thin-film enhancement.
- **`maximum_electron_mobility_cm2_V_s = 4 000 000`** (encapsulated graphene-like device): models predict 1.85×10^5 to 1.0×10^6 — they refuse to commit to the ultra-clean limit even when the description specifies hBN encapsulation and ultra-low-disorder fabrication.
- **`maximum_multi_frequency_loading_rate_s_inv = 1.3×10^11`** (multi-frequency MOT loading): models predict 8.5×10^6 to 4.5×10^9 — off by 1.5 to 4 orders of magnitude.
- **CMS top-quark Bell markers in the asymptotic high-mass / forward bin**: GT values approach zero (e.g. 0.0153, 0.0004) by kinematic cancellation; all four models predict order-unity values, suggesting they are anchoring on the central-bin "prior" rather than the kinematic limit.
- **`tenth_harmonic_zero_delay_second_order_correlation = 7.576`**: predictions split bimodally between ~2 (Poisson prior) and ~50 (BSV prior). No model picked up the harmonic-order dependence.

These are exactly the cases the benchmark *should* be sensitive to, and the "intractable (none correct)" rate of 38 / 296 floats (13 %) is the clearest evidence that the benchmark is measuring more than recall — it's just not yet the dominant contribution to headline scores.

### 3.5 The "easy" tier is mostly ablation noise across model size
On the 72 "easy (1 wrong)" items, gpt-5.4 is right 92 %, gpt-5.4-mini only 54 %. Most of these are within-decade misses by the smaller models, which is consistent with smaller models having a noisier prior rather than fundamentally different physics. The "easy" tier is the bucket that most clearly tracks model size.

## 4. Concrete recommendations

Done in this pass:

- ✅ Switched `analysis/summary.json` to paper-macro of an experiment-macro; `per_model.mean_score` now matches the per-run `aggregate_prediction_quality`.
- ✅ Stripped `±inf` from `numeric_log_accuracy_mean`; the field now has finite values for all four models.
- ✅ **Multi-threshold numeric accuracy** (factor-2, factor-3, factor-10) is now reported in `summary.json::numeric_thresholds` and rendered in `docs/analysis/plots/numeric_thresholds.pdf` and `numeric_log_ratio_cdf.pdf`. The single-cutoff "correct" framing is no longer the only view.
- ✅ **Removed substring-based leakage detection** entirely. The `leak` column is gone from the scored parquet, `leakage_impact` is gone from `summary.json`, and `fig_leakage` is gone from the plot pipeline. See §2.4 for the methodological footnote.
- ✅ **Plot pipeline split into per-paper-macro and per-field-micro views.** `aggregate_paper_macro.pdf` / `per_paper.pdf` / `paper_dominance.pdf` / `numeric_thresholds.pdf` show the headline per-paper view; `aggregate_per_field.pdf` / `difficulty_distribution.pdf` / `numeric_log_ratio_cdf.pdf` / `numeric_scatter.pdf` / `model_agreement.pdf` show the per-field view. `paper_dominance.pdf` now shows both weightings side-by-side so the size of the dominant-paper effect is directly readable.
- ✅ Re-ran `foresight-phys-analysis all`; 33 / 33 tests pass; new `summary.json` and the 9 PDFs in `docs/analysis/plots/` reflect every change above.

Still open, in rough priority order:

1. **Subsample `2602.15115`** (one bin per marker) so it stops being a single paper that re-uses one quantum-information template 60 times. This is the only fix that also reweights the difficulty histogram and the per-field difficulty bucketing.
2. **Add a "definitional vs novel" rubric.** A separate judge call (or the extractor itself) could classify whether the answer is forced by the description (e.g. coherent-state g^(2) = 1) vs requires extrapolation. The current "trivial" bucket conflates these.
3. **Decouple the formula judge from the model family.** Use a non-GPT judge or human spot-check the 7 formula items.
4. **Grow the bool / categorical pool.** 19 bools and 18 categoricals do not give enough power to distinguish models that score 0.84–0.95.
5. **Add a "common-prior" baseline column** (already present as `_constant_baseline_` in `numeric_thresholds`; promote it next to the per-model rows in any HTML rendering).
6. **Reintroduce leak tracking via curator flag**, not substring match. The simplest version is an `extracted_from_description: true` boolean on the ground-truth field, set by the extraction prompt or by human review.

## 5. Verdict

The benchmark **is** measuring more than text recall — the inter-model spread, the per-difficulty separation on the "hard" tier, and the systematic decade-scale failures on intractable items all argue that there is real "physics prior" being probed. But for now the measured signal is dominated by:

- a 35 %-of-fields paper that mostly tests four-models'-agreement on a quantum-information template,
- a generous correctness threshold that makes "matches the textbook order of magnitude" indistinguishable from "predicts the actual measured value",
- bool / categorical questions that, in a majority of cases, restate canonical experimental outcomes.

The hard ~28 % of items (discriminative + hard + intractable) is where the benchmark earns its name, and on that subset the ranking gpt-5.5 > gpt-5.4 > gpt-5.4-nano ≳ gpt-5.4-mini does survive. Reweighting the public report to surface that subset more visibly — and shrinking the dominant paper's contribution — would make the published numbers a much more honest measurement of what the benchmark actually distinguishes.
