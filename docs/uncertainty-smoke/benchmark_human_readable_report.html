Experiment 1

Device-independent two-point-measurement quantum-memory test on a trapped-ion processor. A chain of three ground-state-cooled 40Ca+ ions was confined in a macroscopic linear Paul trap. The two outer ions encoded qubits A′ and E, while the middle ion served only as a spacer to reduce optical cross-talk. Qubit manipulations used an individually addressed 729 nm quadrupole laser with linewidth below 1 Hz, incident at 67.5 degrees to the trap axis; single-ion addressing used a crossed acousto-optic-deflector setup. State readout used resonant 397 nm fluorescence collected onto an EMCCD camera. The two outer ions were initialized in a Bell state with a single Mølmer-Sørensen entangling gate. Qubit A′ was then measured at the first time point. A random variable X with alphabet {0,1,2,3} selected one of four Pauli measurements {+sigma_x,+sigma_z,-sigma_x,-sigma_z}; the binary first outcome was A. During this mid-circuit measurement, qubit E was spectroscopically decoupled by shelving it in unused D5/2 levels, followed by Doppler and polarization-gradient re-cooling. Qubit A was then re-prepared in a state depending only on A and not on X: the quantum-memory test used sigma_x eigenstates |+> and |-> as the two post-measurement states. A variable waiting time was inserted after the first measurement and before the two-qubit unitary; the displayed memory-decay experiment swept the wait time from 0 ms to 100 ms. Magnetic-field noise was mitigated with dynamical-decoupling echo pulses every 2.5 ms during the wait. The subsequent unitary consisted of a CNOT gate followed by a virtual full SWAP of the two qubit labels. The CNOT was implemented as R_C,0(pi/2) · R_T,pi(pi/2) · R_T,pi/4(pi) · MS_XX^{C,T}(pi/2) · R_T,pi/2(pi/2), where R_i,phi(theta) is a single-qubit equatorial rotation of qubit i by angle theta about phase phi, and MS_XX is a Mølmer-Sørensen XX interaction. The second-time binary measurement on qubit B was the observable (sigma_x+sigma_z)/sqrt(2). From the observed conditional probabilities Pr(A=a,B=b|X=x), the classicality parameter Gamma_Pr(AB|X) was computed as the sum over b0,b1 of the infimum over x of the sum over a of Pr(A=a,B=b_a|X=x). The same probability data were also used to evaluate Pearl’s cross-talk witness Delta_Pr(AB|X), defined as the maximum over a of the sum over b of the supremum over x of Pr(A=a,B=b|X=x). Cross-talk was additionally addressed by estimating the average causal direct effect ACDE, the supremum over a,b,x,x′ of the absolute difference between Pr(B_do(A=a,X=x)=b) and Pr(B_do(A=a,X=x′)=b). The qubit-memory fidelity lower bound was inferred from Gamma_Pr(AB|X) using the paper’s device-independent qubit-memory bound.

Experiment 2

Two-point-measurement experiment testing non-classical quantum causal relations with a partial-swap interaction on the same three-ion 40Ca+ trapped-ion processor. The outer ions encoded qubits A′ and E and were initialized in a Bell state using a single Mølmer-Sørensen gate; the middle ion acted as a spacer. At the first time point, qubit A′ was measured with X in {0,1,2,3} selecting the four Pauli observables {+sigma_x,+sigma_z,-sigma_x,-sigma_z}, yielding binary outcome A. Qubit E was shelved during the mid-circuit measurement to suppress measurement back-action, and the ion chain was re-cooled afterward. The post-measurement qubit A was prepared in a sigma_y eigenstate depending only on A and not on X: |+i> for one binary outcome and |-i> for the other, according to the paper’s post-processing convention. Experiment 3

The same 40Ca+ trapped-ion processor was operated with qubit manipulation by the addressed 729 nm laser and readout by 397 nm fluorescence. The same echo pulse used in the memory sequence was also calibrated to estimate its average pulse fidelity.
