Extract the results of an experiment from a physics paper to create a benchmark for evaluating AI models on their ability to predict experimental outcomes from experimental setups. Capture both the paper's central concept (the proposed method, mechanism, regime, or device idea the work demonstrates) and every experiment that establishes or characterizes it.

Requirements:
- Extract distinct experimental procedures and their concrete, empirically measured physical results (dependent variables). When the paper introduces a fabrication, synthesis, growth, transfer, or assembly methodology, treat its primary outcome - whether the procedure reproducibly produces the intended structure - as one of the experiments, alongside each independent characterization, transport, optical, mechanical, spectroscopic, or imaging measurement performed on the resulting samples. Do not silently drop the "did the process work?" experiment in favor of only downstream property measurements.
- ONLY extract INTRINSIC physical or material properties (e.g., bandgaps, symmetries, lattice constants, intrinsic optical/electrical properties) that are fundamentally predictable from the laws of physics and materials science. Reproducible figures-of-merit of fabricated, synthesized, transferred, or assembled structures (e.g., process success/yield, structural integrity, surface roughness, twist-angle homogeneity over a defined area, achievable charge-carrier mobility or mean free path, mechanical resonance frequency of a defined geometry) count as intrinsic when conditioned on a fully specified process and material system.
- STRICTLY EXCLUDE extrinsic, contingent, or random properties. Do not extract random artifact measurements (like the arbitrary thickness of a mechanically exfoliated flake), raw magnitudes highly dependent on unreported variables, or subjective qualitative assessments (e.g., "excellent agreement with theory").
- Every experiment description must be rich and self-contained, in principle allowing the reader to reproduce the experiment and get (hopefully) the same result as you report.
- CRITICAL: The `experiment_description` must contain ALL experimental settings, equipment parameters, fixed conditions, and sweep limits (independent variables).
- CRITICAL: The `experiment_description` must convey the paper's central concept (the proposed method, mechanism, regime, or device idea) wherever it bears on the experiment. A reader without access to the paper should understand why this measurement is being performed and what it is meant to demonstrate. Restate this framing in each `experiment_description` rather than relying on cross-references between experiments.
- CRITICAL: For characterization, imaging, transport, optical, spectroscopic, and mechanical measurements, the `experiment_description` must include the spatial-geometric scope and quality context: the area or scan length over which the measurement applies, the number of independent locations or samples averaged or compared, lateral and depth resolution where relevant, position relative to identifiable features (e.g., the active region, a domain interior, away from edges/defects, on a suspended region), and how surface quality, coverage, continuity, homogeneity, or domain size were assessed. Spatial extent and homogeneity context are first-class parameters, not optional details.
- CRITICAL: For fabrication, synthesis, transfer, growth, or assembly experiments, the `experiment_description` must specify the full process recipe (precursors/source materials and their grade, substrate and its preparation, ambient/atmosphere, temperature and pressure profile, mechanical actions and tooling, timing, and any release or separation mechanism), plus the criteria by which a successful outcome is judged (e.g., target structure identified, coverage exceeds threshold, no observable cracks or wrinkles).
- CRITICAL: Provide explicit physical context for the measurement. If the transport is ballistic, the light is squeezed, or the regime is non-standard (e.g. ultra-low temperature, high-harmonic, large-disorder), state it clearly.
- CRITICAL: Explicitly specify the units, normalization conventions (e.g., SNU, Shot Noise Units relative to vacuum variance), and any specific definitions of markers or metrics (e.g., "entanglement marker Delta_E defined as [definition]") in the description.
- CRITICAL: If a result is likely to be an order-of-magnitude exception to common physical priors (e.g., ultra-high mobility exceeding 1e6 cm2/Vs), ensure the description provides the high-quality environmental details (e.g., encapsulation, vacuum level, substrate purity) that justify such a result.
- CRITICAL: If any `experiment_results` item has `type` = `formula`, the `experiment_description` must explicitly define every variable or symbol used in that formula.
- CRITICAL: The `experiment_description` should NOT contain the measurement results (dependent variables) themselves, so that we can later predict those values from the description without leakage.
- CRITICAL: The `description` field within `experiment_results` items MUST NOT contain the result values, their magnitudes, or specific references to the findings (e.g., do not say "reported as 5.0 eV"). Use it only to define what was measured.
- Every description should name the material or device, the measurement modality or instrument, the relevant sample geometry or thickness when available, the environmental or operating conditions, the swept control variables, and exactly how the reported result was obtained.
- Avoid vague references such as "this sample", "the above device", "the measured spectrum", or "as shown in Fig. 2".
- CRITICAL: The `experiment_results` section MUST ONLY contain the actual observed physical measurements, derived empirical properties, or dependent variables (e.g., measured resistance, photovoltage, responsivity, transition temperature, process success indicator or yield, surface RMS roughness, twist-angle spread over a defined region, lateral domain size, mechanical resonance frequency and quality factor, charge-carrier mobility, mean free path).
- NEVER put human choices, independent variables, or equipment settings (e.g., applied voltage, sweep limit, laser frequency, lock-in frequency) in the `experiment_results`. These belong in the `experiment_description`.
- Only include results grounded in the paper text, tables, captions, or clearly legible plotted values. Do not invent hidden values or unnecessary precision.
- Prefer scalar targets that can be predicted independently later.
- Use snake_case values for each experiment_results item key.
- Populate experiment_results as a list of result entries, one entry per predicted field.
- Encode units or key conditions in result keys when they help disambiguate the target (e.g., `voltage_responsivity_kV_W`).
- Use `type` = `formula` for algebraic or functional expressions in `result`. Do NOT use `string` as a result type.
- If a single experimental setup yields several independently reportable targets, keep them in the same experiment_results object.
- Exclude pure background facts, literature values cited from other work, and claims with no specific observable tied to a described measurement.
